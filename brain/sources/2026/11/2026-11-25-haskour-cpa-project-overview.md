Haskour CPA - Project Overview

Nov 25, 2026 · @Camille

Overview

Haskour CPA needs a refreshed brand, a premium website and a Microsoft-based client intake form, all live before tax season opens on 1 March 2027.

The firm is a registered tax accountant about to receive its CPA registration. That changes the firm's name and is the reason for the brand refresh.

The project has three parts, plus a marketing push for the final weeks of February:

1.  Brand refresh. The new CPA name and a slight logo refresh.

2.  Website. A premium, simple site that makes tax feel less complicated.

3.  Intake and automation. The Canadian personal tax questionnaire as a Microsoft Form with document upload, behind an email login, feeding SharePoint.

The client and who they serve

Haskour CPA has about 400 clients today and wants to keep every one of them while building a long lifeline of new ones.

The client base breaks into three groups, in order of size:

1.  Canadian personal tax. The majority of clients.

2.  US taxpayers living in Canada. The niche market to target.

3.  Corporate A smaller group.

Tax is complex and many people find it stressful. The brand, the website and the intake experience all need to make it feel simple.

Brand refresh

The brand work is a name change and a slight logo refresh, not a rebrand from scratch.

The firm's name changes once the CPA registration comes through. The logo should be refreshed to match the new name and sit comfortably beside a more premium website.

The new name is due within about 2 weeks. This is still to be confirmed with the client.

Website

The main deliverable is a new website that looks premium and expensive but is simple and easy to navigate.

The client likes the layout of mnp.ca as a reference. Tax feels complicated to most people, so the site should do the opposite: clear paths, plain language and very little clutter.

The client portal stays external. The firm pays for specialised tax software and all current clients already live there, so the website links out to it rather than replacing it.

Intake form

The client's intake questionnaire, based on the Canadian personal tax form, becomes a Microsoft Form. The client's whole ecosystem lives in Microsoft and uses Copilot, so he requested the form sit natively inside it.

The launch scope is one form only. More forms can follow later.

What the form needs

1.  Questions that follow the Canadian personal tax form.

2.  A document upload option for tax slips and supporting files.

3.  Access behind an email wall, so the form is not open to anyone who lands on the website.

The email gate does two jobs. It protects the client's intellectual property and it captures the email of anyone who uses the form.

To confirm with Madz: whether the Microsoft Forms file upload feature can be done easily with built-in settings or needs custom coding.

Back-end automation

The client already keeps his forms and tax slips in SharePoint and wants that workflow automated.

The goal is for completed forms and uploaded tax slips to land in the right SharePoint location without manual filing. The client's ecosystem is in Microsoft, so the build needs to fit inside what they already run.

This is the AI automation side of the project, priced separately from the website.

Is this something Madz would do?

Marketing and content

The client wants to invest a little in marketing, built from three pieces.

1.  Video package. The Green Horizon 10 video package, already scoped.

2.  Monthly thought leadership. One newsletter article each month, distilled into 5 social posts for LinkedIn and Instagram. Camille schedules all posts a month ahead, at the start of each month.

3.  Photography. A shoot in January once the client's new office building is ready. This comes after the main build.

The campaign push runs across Instagram and LinkedIn for the last 2 weeks of February, so the firm has a clean start for tax season.

Timeline

The kickoff is on 7 December 2026 and the brand, website and Microsoft Form automation are finished by 15 February 2027.
